# Core-Centered Construction WF Selection Design

## Decision and scope

The user approved selecting construction WFs whose centers R lie inside the
owning fragment's core, followed by core-metric independence and WF+PW
reconstruction checks. R means the actual localized WF center, not an atom
position, candidate label, or Cartesian direction.

This amendment supersedes the unconditional **production** all-WF-retention
rule in the September 2 divided-SCF design and Tasks 5/8. The raw construction
cache still retains every generated WF. It does not authorize deleting raw
data, forcing WF symmetry, changing the MPI layout, repeating DC, increasing
the PW cutoff automatically, or switching the production main route before
the remaining Task 8 gates pass.

Use the existing worktree only:
`/Users/otobetoshihito/SALMON-dev/SALMON2_RTDG/.worktrees/wpw-s-orthogonal-complement`.
One rank per fragment and exact rank-count/rank--fragment DC reuse remain
permanent. All pre-existing dirty changes and verification logs are retained.

## Why selection is separate from construction

The opt-in diagnostic introduced at `4663e330` shows that independence on
core+buffer does not imply independence on core. In its two-fragment fixture,
two auxiliary candidates per fragment live only on buffer grid points 7/8;
their core norms vanish. A unitary localization cannot remove this null
combination. The current preconditioner rejects it before CG.

This is an artificial counterexample, not a diagnosis of material WFs. Center
selection is a physically motivated catalog choice, not a proof of positive
metric rank or sufficient span. Selected WFs can still become dependent after
restriction; excluded WFs can have important tails inside core.

Three alternatives were considered: keeping every buffer WF (current problem),
center-only selection with no verification (no accuracy/rank assurance), and
center selection with metric and reconstruction verification (approved).

## Raw cache and selected catalog

Maintain three distinct objects:

1. Immutable raw fragment cache: all localized values on the accepted buffer,
   candidate compression, final phase/order-corrected Wannier transform U,
   matching centers, original seed coefficient map, spectrum and occupations.
2. Selected fragment catalog: stable raw-column IDs selected by center, their
   unchanged values on all original buffer rows, and a selection receipt.
   Subsequent WF+PW matrices/CG use this catalog, not raw-cache counts.
3. Terminal complete catalog: the existing separately fingerprinted map from
   the selected WF+PW union used only for final LCFO. It is not a local CG map.

The current fragment wrapper obtains centers, converts them to fragment
fractional coordinates and passes them through `apply_dg_w90_gamma_transform`,
which can reorder centers, transform columns and values together. Persist
centers **after** that call. Include them in replicated cache integrity and
collective validation. Do not infer centers from column order or parse a
possibly stale `.wout` file. No raw cache with absent/corrupt centers is eligible
for selection; do not silently rerun W90 on the same generation/artifacts.

## Center coordinates and ownership

Selection uses the same physical origin, periodic cell, core box and grid
convention as the DC physical mapping and production windows. The initial
implementation supports the existing axis-aligned production fragment boxes;
it must reject an inconsistent or unsupported geometry, not guess its origin.

Map final fragment-fractional centers through the construction-cell lattice
and its actual raw-grid origin into the total physical cell. Validate this
continuous mapping against `dc%jxyz_tot`, including periodic wraps and the raw
core-first layout. Do not assume the core is centered inside the buffer.

Core ownership is half-open, `[lower,upper)`, along each dimension with
periodic wrapping. A center on a shared face/edge/corner has exactly one owner;
the total-cell upper face wraps to its lower face. Canonicalize floating-point
roundoff near a boundary using one geometry-scaled, versioned tolerance and
the same snapped boundary on both sides, not independently inflated boxes.
The tolerance must be roundoff-scale, not the WF spread/localization tolerance.
Record the convention and geometry in the selection fingerprint.

For the cache generated by fragment f, select a raw column only if its mapped
center belongs to core f. Foreign-centered WFs are excluded from this local
catalog, not transferred to another rank: independently constructed fragments
are not assumed to provide identical foreign WFs. Preserve raw order among
selected columns; do not renumber raw provenance IDs. Do not cap the count or
select by auxiliary W90 eigenvalues. All WFs sharing an admitted center are
eligible; center coincidence alone is not a reason to remove one.

A zero-WF selection is a valid selector result; downstream WF+PW construction
must either support a certified PW-only fragment or reject it explicitly before
publication. This amendment does not silently turn on a new PW-only fallback.

## Tails, PW projection and admission

Selection removes columns from the active catalog, not buffer rows from an
accepted column. Keep values needed for derivative evaluation, face traces,
periodic wraps and projector-support coverage. Physical density remains
uniquely core-owned. No tapering or value clipping is introduced.

Build the generalized PW complement against the **selected WF union**, not
the discarded raw WF union. Otherwise the projection can remove precisely the
PW components needed to replace excluded tails. The user cutoff retains its
current units/meaning and is never raised invisibly.

Assemble the actual core-volume S and volume/interface/nonlocal H in these
selected WF+PW coordinates. Require positive resolved local metric rank and
adequate seed/state capacity before local preconditioner publication. Do not
declare S valid from diagonal norms alone. A remaining null or unresolved mode
is a collective diagnostic failure in this increment; automatic local
eigenspace compression is not authorized by center selection. In particular,
do not feed the terminal union compression into the local loop or substitute
identity/buffer S.

For seed orbitals Psi and the admitted basis B, reconstruct coefficients from
the core normal equations `S C = B^dagger W_core Psi`. This is a local metric
solve, not a complete-system Hamiltonian diagonalization. Merely slicing old
coefficients or zero-padding them with PW coefficients is generally wrong
after selection. Preserve the original raw seed map as the reconstruction
oracle. With B=all raw WFs and no lost span the new map must agree physically
with the prior map.

Record separately the weighted orbital residual, occupied density/electron
defect, and required boundary/derivative/projector reconstruction errors.
Use declared existing seed/orthogonality/electron and support tolerances; do
not loosen them implicitly. Verify density before and after initialization
with its actual coefficient/occupation transformations: independent column
renormalization with unchanged occupations is not evidence of seed-density
preservation. Lossless reconstruction of discarded buffer-only data is not
required, but loss of a required boundary/projector contribution is not waived.

For this increment the post-initializer density check is **fail-only
admission**, not an implementation of a density-preserving occupation
transformation. The current initializer S-orthonormalizes coefficients and
does not return transformed occupations. An extended-domain DC orbital can
have core norm 1/2; normalizing it on core with unchanged occupation doubles
its core density. Multiple nonorthogonal core orbitals can require a general
occupation matrix to preserve the same density. Therefore add this explicit
negative test and reject an initializer handoff that exceeds the declared
density/electron tolerances. Do not relabel it as an insufficient PW cutoff
when the pre-initializer projection passed. Preserve the valid raw cache and
diagnostics and publish no accepted solver state for the failed handoff.

No general occupation-matrix/natural-orbital machinery or revised SCF starting
density is introduced by this amendment. If representative small DC inputs
need such handling, stop at the C3 checkpoint and design it explicitly before
promoting C5/C6; tests that only have orthogonal unit-core-norm seeds cannot
establish a general DC handoff. C1/C2 are independent of this admission issue.

If the user cutoff cannot represent an excluded but significant tail, fail
with the measured errors, selected counts and cutoff. Do not automatically
re-admit foreign-centered WFs, conceal the defect by density rescaling, or
claim DG communication/PW presence guarantees accuracy. Any future adaptive
cutoff/selection policy requires a separate decision.

## Fixed-reference preconditioning after selection

Selection is not a square unitary gauge change. Let `B_raw=F_raw U` and E
contain the selected identity columns. Then `B_sel=B_raw E`. The existing
square `U^dagger` cannot simply be sliced and passed to an API that requires a
unitary square matrix.

For this design use the fixed projected reference frame
`Q_sel=E^dagger U^dagger`, and `Q=block_diag(Q_sel,I_PW)` after PW augmentation.
Q can be rectangular and satisfies `Q Q^dagger=I`; in general
`Q^dagger Q != I`. The physical frame is `F_proj=B Q`. Do not describe it as
the original, exactly retained F_raw; it is fixed for this **selected** basis
generation. Bind the selection, Q and PW reference into its provenance.

Use the same shifted-frame action:

```text
h_a = q_a^dagger H_ff q_a
s_a = q_a^dagger S_ff q_a
z_j = sum_a q_a (h_a - epsilon_j s_a)_regularized^-1 q_a^dagger r_j
```

Omit only reference columns whose coefficient norm is zero at the documented
roundoff threshold, and verify that the remaining frame still resolves the
identity within tolerance. A nonzero reference column with unresolved metric
norm is an admission failure, not permission to relax positivity. Keep the
existing signed denominator floor, current Rayleigh shifts and epoch checks.
Preserve the old square API semantics; add an explicit rectangular-frame entry
with its own validation rather than weakening old tests.

This is a proposed implementation detail of the approved selection policy,
to be independently reviewed before coding its task. Tests must show the
square case reduces to the old action and, for a unitary V **inside the frozen
selected space**, `B'=B V`, `Q'=V^dagger Q` gives the same physical update.
Arbitrary rotation mixing selected and excluded raw WFs changes the selection
subspace and is no longer an invariance requirement. Phase/permutation of raw
WFs with correctly transported centers must leave the selected physical span
unchanged. No matching gauge is imposed between fragments.

## Validation and release boundary

### Approved signed-cancellation safeguard

Independent C4 review found that a redundant rectangular frame can cancel
positive and negative inverse-denominator contributions even when all metric
norms are positive. The user approved retaining the signed rule and stopping
explicitly on detected cancellation, not replacing the action by identity or
an unsigned inverse. For signed-scaled reference amplitudes y, reject when
`||Q y||_2 <= tau * sum_a ||q_a||_2 |y_a|` with a nonzero right-hand term sum.
Use `tau=max(tolerance,64*epsilon_machine*max(active_count,reference_count))`.
Scale amplitudes before forming norms; an identically zero term sum is not an
error. The gate concerns the current applied residual, not global invertibility.
It assumes the separate producer has certified the frame and its provenance.
The old square entry retains its existing contract. Rectangular application
must run this check before accepting output; the stand-alone numerical gate
alone does not complete C4 or authorize production use.

The rectangular numerical entry is single-owner (fragment communicator size
one), consistent with ranks equalling fragments. Define roundoff-zero column
norm as `64*epsilon_machine*max(active_count,input_reference_count)` and
recheck identity resolution after omission. Use the retained reference count
in the signed denominator roundoff dimension and cancellation check. Bind the
original frame payload, selection and retained count in the cache receipt.
Input validation must not test metric norms in an artificial identity frame;
the actual retained reference columns define those tests. Full core-metric
positive-rank admission remains the separate C3 requirement.

Test centers and their final ordering first, then geometry/selection, selected
PW construction and seed projection, rectangular preconditioning, and the
actual small DG operator handoff. Preserve the old unselected core-metric
counterexample as a specifically asserted negative case. Add a selected-path
positive case with physically consistent center/value fixtures; the old W90
stub's arbitrary centers cannot serve as physical evidence.

No claim of improved material accuracy, symmetry certification or completed
Task 8 follows from these unit tests. Only after all focused gates pass may
main's separate direct-DC entry consume the selected catalog. Final LCFO
symmetry/window and eventual RT checks remain unchanged; the expensive
material comparison remains Task 11 and reuses the saved DC result.

## Design review checkpoint

Independent review found the center/catalog separation and rectangular-frame
covariance algebra consistent. It identified the post-initializer occupation
gap; this revision explicitly limits C3 to fail-only density admission and
requires another design checkpoint if preserving general DC density needs
occupation transformations. Geometry validation must receive the actual raw
DC index mapping. C1 is ready to implement; general selected-path production
acceptance is not yet established.
